Chapter 7 covers the male reproductive system. Function: reproduction — providing half the genetic information. Specialties: Andrology (male reproductive medicine) and Urology (urologist). The testis has FOUR combining forms (orchid/o, orchi/o, orch/o, test/o). Most forgotten CF: balan/o = glans penis.

Parenchymal vs stromal tissue
Parenchymal
Gonads (testes) — the workers
Only the GONADS (testes, gonad/o) are parenchymal — they do the functional work. Two roles: (1) produce SPERMATOZOA in seminiferous tubules (spermatogenesis); (2) produce TESTOSTERONE from Leydig cells (endocrine). Gametes = reproductive cells (sperm).
Stromal
Glands, ducts, nerves
All other structures support the gonads: scrotum (scrot/o), epididymis (epididym/o), vas deferens (vas/o), spermatic cord, seminal vesicles (vesicul/o), prostate (prostat/o), bulbourethral glands, ejaculatory duct, penis (pen/i, phall/o), glans penis (balan/o), prepuce (preputi/o).
A&P terms
TermDefinition
Coitus / CopulationSexual intercourse. Three terms used interchangeably: coitus = copulation = sexual intercourse. Conception = fertilization of an ovum by a spermatozoon. coitus from Latin coïre = to come together.
Ductus deferensThe vas deferens (vas/o) — muscular tube carrying sperm from epididymis toward ejaculatory duct. ~45 cm. Part of the spermatic cord. Site of vasectomy (surgical cutting for sterilization). Also called vas deferens.
EpididymisCoiled tube (epididym/o) on posterior surface of each testis. Functions: sperm maturation (~12 days to gain motility) and storage until ejaculation. Epididymitis = epididym/o + -itis = inflammation.
Foreskin / PrepuceFold of skin (preputi/o, posth/o) covering glans penis. Removed in circumcision (circum- + -cision). Phimosis = non-retractable prepuce. Paraphimosis = retracted prepuce cannot return = emergency.
GametesReproductive cells (sex cells). Male gamete = spermatozoon (plural: spermatozoa). Each carries 23 chromosomes (haploid). Fertilization restores 46 chromosomes (diploid). Produced in gonads by meiosis.
Glans penisRounded distal tip of penis (balan/o). Contains external urethral meatus. Covered by prepuce in uncircumcised males. Balanitis = balan/o + -itis = glans inflammation. Most commonly FORGOTTEN CF in Chapter 7.
GonadsReproductive glands (gonad/o) — parenchymal tissue. Male gonads = testes. Dual function: gamete production (spermatogenesis) AND sex hormone secretion (testosterone). Regulated by FSH and LH from pituitary.
PenisMale external organ (pen/i, phall/o). Contains urethra. Three erectile bodies: two corpora cavernosa + one corpus spongiosum (surrounds urethra). Erection = blood fills erectile tissue. Male urethra = 15-20 cm.
ProstateWalnut-sized gland (prostat/o) surrounding urethra at bladder base. Produces alkaline prostatic fluid (~30% of semen), nourishes and protects sperm, produces PSA. BPH = benign enlargement compressing urethra.
ScrotumExternal skin pouch (scrot/o) containing testes. Maintains testes at ~34°C (2-3°C below body temp) — required for spermatogenesis. Cremasteric reflex: cold/touch → cremaster muscle pulls testes upward.
SemenFluid ejaculated at orgasm (semin/i). Composition: spermatozoa + seminal vesicle secretions (~60%) + prostatic fluid (~30%) + bulbourethral (~5%). Volume: 2-5 mL. Alkaline pH (7.2-8.0) neutralizes vaginal acidity.
Seminal vesiclesPaired glands (vesicul/o) posterior to bladder. Produce ~60% of semen — rich in fructose (energy for sperm) and prostaglandins. Vesiculitis = vesicul/o + -itis = seminal vesicle inflammation.
Seminiferous tubulesTiny coiled tubes (~250 m uncoiled) inside each testis where spermatogenesis occurs. Sertoli cells support developing sperm. Leydig cells (between tubules) produce testosterone.
Spermatic cordBundle from deep inguinal ring to testis: vas deferens + testicular artery + pampiniform plexus veins + lymphatics + nerves. Testicular torsion = cord twists → surgical emergency (6-hour window).
SpermatogenesisProcess (spermat/o + -genesis = production) of sperm production in seminiferous tubules. Begins at puberty, continuous. Regulated by FSH and testosterone. Takes ~74 days. Requires 34°C.
SpermatozoonA single mature sperm cell (spermat/o, sperm/o). Plural: spermatozoa. Head (DNA + acrosome) + midpiece (mitochondria) + flagellum/tail (motility). Azoospermia = no sperm; oligospermia = too few.
TestisMale gonad (test/o, orchid/o, orchi/o, orch/o). Plural: testes. Dual function: spermatogenesis (in seminiferous tubules) + testosterone production (Leydig cells). Must be ~34°C. Undescended = cryptorchidism.
TestosteronePrimary male sex hormone (androgen) produced by Leydig cells. Functions: male secondary sex characteristics, spermatogenesis, libido, muscle mass, bone density. Regulated by LH from anterior pituitary.
Detailed anatomy of each male reproductive structure with combining forms, clinical connections, and the sperm production process.
Testis structure
Seminiferous tubules
Where sperm are made
~250 m of coiled tubes per testis. Spermatogenesis occurs here (~74 days). SERTOLI CELLS (nurse cells): support developing sperm, form blood-testis barrier, produce inhibin (suppresses FSH). Regulated by FSH.
Leydig cells
Where testosterone is made
Interstitial cells BETWEEN tubules. Stimulated by LH → produce TESTOSTERONE → male secondary sex characteristics, spermatogenesis support, libido. Androgen hormone inhibitors (finasteride) block testosterone action.
Temperature regulation
Why testes are in the scrotum
Spermatogenesis requires ~34°C (2-3° below body temp). Cryptorchidism = testis stays in abdomen at 37°C → no spermatogenesis → infertility. Cremasteric reflex adjusts position with temperature.
Sperm maturation path
Epididymis (epididym/o)
Maturation and storage (~6 m coiled)
Sperm spend ~12 days gaining motility and fertilizing ability. Head, body, tail segments. Stores mature sperm until ejaculation. Epididymitis = inflammation: chlamydia/gonorrhea (<35 yrs), E. coli (>35 yrs).
Vas deferens (vas/o)
Transport tube (~45 cm)
Muscular tube carrying sperm from epididymis to ejaculatory duct. Part of spermatic cord. VASECTOMY = cutting vas deferens = male sterilization. VASOVASOSTOMY = reconnects cut ends = vasectomy reversal.
Seminal vesicles (vesicul/o)
60% of semen volume
Produce fructose (energy for sperm), prostaglandins, coagulating proteins. Join vas deferens → form ejaculatory duct. vesicul/o (seminal vesicle) ≠ vesic/o (urinary bladder, Ch.6).
Prostate gland (prostat/o)
30% of semen volume
Surrounds urethra at bladder base. Alkaline fluid neutralizes vaginal acidity; contains PSA. BPH = enlargement compressing urethra → urinary symptoms (urgency, weak stream, retention). Rx: alpha-blockers, TURP.
Penis structure
pen/i, phall/o
Corpora cavernosa (×2)
Two paired erectile cylinders. Parasympathetic stimulation → arterioles dilate → sinusoids fill with blood → erection. Erectile dysfunction (ED) = inability to achieve or maintain erection sufficient for intercourse.
Corpus spongiosum
Contains the urethra
Single erectile cylinder surrounding the urethra. Expands distally to form the GLANS PENIS (balan/o) — the rounded tip. Contains the external urethral meatus.
Prepuce / Foreskin (preputi/o)
Covers the glans
Fold of skin covering glans penis in uncircumcised males. Circumcision (circum- + -cision) = surgical removal. Phimosis = prepuce too tight to retract. Paraphimosis = retracted prepuce cannot return = emergency.
The four testis combining forms
orchid/o
Greek — full form
Orchidectomy (testis removal), cryptorchidism (hidden testis). Full Greek form — most used in compound terms. The orchid flower was named for its testis-shaped tubers (Greek orchis = testis).
orchi/o
Greek — short form
Orchiopexy (surgical fixation), orchiectomy (alternate testis removal). Commonly used interchangeably with orchid/o in clinical texts.
orch/o
Greek — shortest form
Orchitis (orch/o + -itis = testis inflammation). Shortest form — seen in textbooks for brevity. Less common in compound terms.
test/o
Latin — for adjectives and hormones
Testicular (pertaining to testis), testosterone (the testis hormone), testitis (testis inflammation). Latin origin used in anatomical/adjectival terms.

⚠ Key suffix distinctions
-cision vs -ectomy
circum-CISION = the act of CUTTING around (the cutting technique itself). prostat-ECTOMY = REMOVAL of the prostate (an organ is excised). -cision describes the cutting action; -ectomy describes the surgical removal outcome.
-stomy vs -tomy
vasovasoSTOMY = creates a NEW PERMANENT OPENING/CONNECTION between two vas deferens ends. nephroTOMY = an INCISION into the kidney (temporary cut, no new permanent opening). -stomy makes a lasting connection; -tomy just cuts.
hypo-spadias vs epi-spadias
hypo-SPADIAS: urethral opening on UNDERSIDE (ventral/bottom) of penis. epi-SPADIAS: opening on TOPSIDE (dorsal/above). hypo- = below; epi- = upon/above. Both are congenital urethral placement errors.
-genesis vs -genic
spermatogeneSIS = the PROCESS of sperm production (noun — the event itself). spermatoGENIC = PERTAINING TO producing sperm (adjective — the quality). -genesis = a process (noun); -genic = producing (adjective).

Clinical terms organized by category. Chapter 7 has high-yield congenital disorders, STIs, -itis terms, and the critical BPH/prostate disease cluster.
Congenital disorders
Cryptorchidism
crypt- + orchid/o + -ism
Undescended testis — fails to descend into scrotum. Risks: infertility (heat → no spermatogenesis), increased testicular cancer risk (6×). Rx: orchiopexy before age 2.
Anorchism
an- + orch/o + -ism
Absence of one (monorchism) or both testes. Congenital or surgical. Bilateral = no testosterone → hormone replacement needed.
Hypospadias
hypo- + -spadias
Urethral meatus on UNDERSIDE (ventral) of penis. Congenital. Range: near tip to base. Rx: surgical urethroplasty. Opposite: epispadias (epi- = meatus on TOP).
Epispadias
epi- + -spadias
Urethral meatus on TOPSIDE (dorsal) of penis. Less common than hypospadias. Often associated with bladder exstrophy. epi- = upon/above.
Phimosis
preputi/o — tight foreskin
Non-retractable prepuce (foreskin too tight). Causes recurrent infections, urinary obstruction. Rx: topical steroids or circumcision. Paraphimosis = retracted prepuce trapped = emergency.
Chordee
congenital penile curvature
Congenital downward curvature of penis, often with hypospadias. Fibrous tissue replacing normal corpus spongiosum. Can cause painful erections. Rx: surgical correction.
Sperm & semen disorders
Azoospermia
a- + zo/o + sperm/o + -ia
COMPLETE ABSENCE of sperm in semen. Obstructive (blocked vas/epididymis) or non-obstructive (spermatogenesis failure). Diagnosis: semen analysis. Most severe male infertility.
Oligospermia
olig/o + sperm/o + -ia
Low sperm count (<15 million/mL). Most common cause of male infertility. Varicocele = #1 correctable cause (raises scrotal temperature). Aspermia = no semen ejaculate at all.
Aspermia
a- + sperm/o + -ia
Complete absence of semen (ejaculate). Different from azoospermia (no sperm in semen that is present). Causes: retrograde ejaculation, ejaculatory duct obstruction.
Structural disorders
Testicular torsion
spermatic cord twists
SURGICAL EMERGENCY — 6-hour window. Sudden severe scrotal pain, high-riding testis, ABSENT cremasteric reflex. Rx: emergency detorsion + bilateral orchiopexy (prevents future torsion on both sides).
Varicocele
varic/o + -cele
Dilated varicose veins of spermatic cord. “Bag of worms.” More common on LEFT. #1 CORRECTABLE cause of male infertility — raises scrotal temperature. Rx: varicocelectomy.
Hydrocele
hydr/o + -cele
Fluid accumulation in tunica vaginalis → scrotal swelling. Most common cause of painless scrotal swelling in newborns. Transilluminates (light passes through). Usually resolves spontaneously.
Erectile dysfunction (ED)
inability to achieve erection
Inability to achieve or maintain erection. Most common cause: vascular (atherosclerosis). Rx: PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil/Viagra, tadalafil/Cialis). Priapism = painful erection >4 hrs = emergency.
Gynecomastia
gynec/o + mast/o + -ia
Benign male breast tissue enlargement. Estrogen/testosterone imbalance (puberty, liver disease, medications, testicular tumors). Usually bilateral. gynec/o (woman) + mast/o (breast).
Inflammation (-itis terms)
Balanitis
balan/o + -itis
Glans penis inflammation. Common in uncircumcised males. Most common cause: Candida. Rx: antifungal/antibiotics, improved hygiene; circumcision if recurrent.
Epididymitis
epididym/o + -itis
Epididymis inflammation. <35 yrs: chlamydia/gonorrhea. >35 yrs: E. coli. Gradual onset, fever, dysuria. ⚠ Must distinguish from testicular torsion (emergency). Rx: antibiotics.
Orchitis / Testitis
orch/o + -itis / test/o + -itis
Testis inflammation. Viral: mumps orchitis in 15-25% of postpubertal males → can cause infertility. Bacterial: usually from epididymis spread. Rx: treat underlying cause.
Prostatitis
prostat/o + -itis
Prostate inflammation. Types: acute bacterial (emergency — fever, systemic illness, perineal pain, dysuria), chronic bacterial, chronic pelvic pain syndrome (most common). Rx: antibiotics.
Vesiculitis
vesicul/o + -itis
Seminal vesicle inflammation. Usually occurs with prostatitis/epididymitis. Symptoms: hematospermia (blood in ejaculate), painful ejaculation, perineal pain. Rx: antibiotics.
Prostate disorders
BPH
hyper- + -plasia = excessive prostate growth
Benign prostatic hyperplasia — non-cancerous enlargement compressing urethra. LUTS: urgency, frequency, nocturia, weak stream, retention. Affects majority of men over 60. Rx: tamsulosin, finasteride, TURP.
PSA
prostate-specific antigen
Blood test measuring PSA protein produced by prostate cells. Elevated in BPH (mildly), prostatitis (significantly), prostate cancer. Normal: <4 ng/mL. Used for prostate cancer screening (controversial).
Adenocarcinoma of prostate
most common male cancer
Most common cancer in men (excluding skin cancer). Arises from peripheral zone of prostate. Graded by Gleason score. Screening: DRE + PSA. Rx: active surveillance, prostatectomy, radiation, hormonal therapy.
Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
Gonorrhea
Neisseria gonorrhoeae
Bacterial STI. Symptoms in males: purulent urethral discharge, dysuria. Can cause epididymitis. Dx: NAAT or Gram stain (gram-negative diplococci). Rx: ceftriaxone + doxycycline (dual therapy).
NGU (nongonococcal urethritis)
non- + gonococcal + urethr/o + -itis
Urethral inflammation NOT from gonorrhea. Most common cause: Chlamydia trachomatis (50%). Often asymptomatic. Rx: doxycycline or azithromycin. non- = NOT gonococcal.
Syphilis
Treponema pallidum
Stages: primary (painless chancre) → secondary (palms/soles rash) → latent → tertiary (neurosyphilis, aortitis). Dx: VDRL (screening), FTA-ABS (confirmatory). Rx: penicillin G (all stages).
HPV
human papillomavirus — most common STI
Most common STI. Low-risk types (6, 11): genital warts. High-risk types (16, 18): cervical, anal, penile, oropharyngeal cancers. Prevention: Gardasil 9 vaccine (ages 9-26, extend to 45). No cure; treat manifestations.
Diagnostic procedures, therapeutic interventions, and pharmacology for the male reproductive system.
Diagnostic procedures
ProcedureDefinition & notes
DRE (digital rectal exam)Palpation of prostate through rectal wall using gloved finger. Assesses prostate size, consistency, nodules. Abnormal: asymmetry, nodules, induration → suspicious for cancer. Done with PSA testing.
PSA (prostate-specific antigen)Blood test. Elevated in BPH, prostatitis, prostate cancer. Normal: <4 ng/mL. Screening value is controversial (overdiagnosis debate). Additional metrics: PSA velocity, PSA density, free/total PSA ratio improve specificity.
Transrectal sonographyUltrasound probe via rectum → images prostate. Guides prostate needle biopsy for suspected cancer. Estimates prostate volume for BPH staging. Evaluates seminal vesicles.
Sperm analysis (semen analysis)Analyzes ejaculate: volume (≥1.5 mL), count (≥15 million/mL), motility (≥40%), morphology (≥4% normal forms). Diagnoses azoospermia, oligospermia, and other causes of male infertility.
Penile plethysmographyplethysm/o + -graphy = recording of penile blood volume changes. Assesses erectile response for ED evaluation. Rigiscan: nocturnal tumescence monitoring — distinguishes psychological vs physiological ED.
TSE (testicular self-exam)Monthly self-examination of testes for lumps/changes. Primary screening for testicular cancer — most common cancer in males ages 15-35. Technique: warm shower (relaxes scrotum), gently roll each testis between thumb and fingers.
VDRL & FTA-ABSSyphilis tests. VDRL = screening (non-specific). FTA-ABS = confirmatory (specific for Treponema pallidum antibodies). Gram stain: identifies gonorrhea (gram-negative intracellular diplococci) from urethral discharge.
Therapeutic procedures
ProcedureDefinition & notes
Vasectomyvas/o + -ectomy = surgical CUTTING/REMOVAL of vas deferens segment → male sterilization. Outpatient, local anesthesia. Does NOT affect testosterone, libido, or ejaculate volume (sperm = ~5% of semen). >99.9% effective.
Vasovasostomyvas/o + vas/o + -stomy = NEW OPENING connecting the two cut vas ends = vasectomy REVERSAL. Microsurgical anastomosis. Success: ~90% if <3 years; ~30% if >15 years post-vasectomy. Semen analysis confirms success.
TURPTransurethral resection of prostate — trans- (through) + urethr/o + resection. Gold standard BPH surgery. Resectoscope via urethra → no external incision. Removes obstructing tissue. Side effects: retrograde ejaculation (most common), impotence.
TUIPTransurethral incision of prostate — makes incisions in prostate/bladder neck rather than removing tissue. For smaller prostates. Lower retrograde ejaculation rate than TURP.
Prostatectomyprostat/o + -ectomy = prostate REMOVAL. Radical (entire prostate + seminal vesicles + lymph nodes) for cancer. Robotic-assisted laparoscopic (RALP) most common approach. Side effects: incontinence, erectile dysfunction.
Orchidectomy / Orchiectomyorchid/o + -ectomy = testis REMOVAL. Radical INGUINAL approach (via groin) for testicular cancer. Bilateral = castration → no testosterone. Castration also used for advanced prostate cancer treatment.
Orchiopexyorchi/o + -pexy = surgical FIXATION of undescended testis into scrotum. For cryptorchidism, ideally before age 2. Also performed bilaterally after testicular torsion to prevent recurrence on other side.
Circumcisioncircum- + -cision = surgical removal of prepuce (foreskin). Most common neonatal surgical procedure in US. Benefits: reduced UTI, STI (HIV, HPV), phimosis, penile cancer. Also religious/cultural practice.
Pharmacology
Alpha-adrenergic inhibitors
Relax smooth muscle of prostate and bladder neck → reduce BPH urinary symptoms. Examples: tamsulosin (Flomax), doxazosin. First-line for symptomatic BPH. Work quickly (days). Side effect: orthostatic hypotension.
Androgen hormone inhibitors
5-alpha reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) block testosterone → DHT conversion → reduce prostate size over months. For BPH and prostate cancer. Side effects: sexual dysfunction, gynecomastia.
Antiimpotence agents (PDE-5 inhibitors)
Sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil (Cialis), vardenafil. Block PDE-5 → increased cGMP → penile smooth muscle relaxation → erection (requires sexual stimulation). ⚠ Contraindicated with nitrates (severe hypotension).
Antibiotics & antivirals
STI treatment: ceftriaxone (gonorrhea), doxycycline/azithromycin (chlamydia/NGU), penicillin G (syphilis). Antivirals: acyclovir, valacyclovir (herpes genitalis). Antifungals: clotrimazole, miconazole (balanitis from Candida).
