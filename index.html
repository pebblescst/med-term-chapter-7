Functions of the Male Reproductive System
Sperm production
Spermatogenesis in seminiferous tubules of the testes
Hormone production
Testosterone secreted by Leydig cells of the testes
Delivery of sperm
Transport and delivery of spermatozoa to female reproductive tract during coitus
Semen formation
Seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbourethral glands contribute to semen
Conception
Fertilization of ovum by spermatozoon — conception
Urinary passage
Urethra (urethr/o) serves as shared pathway for urine and semen
Specialty: Urology · Specialist: Urologist  |  Gonads = testes (produce gametes and hormones)  |  Gametes = sex cells (spermatozoa)  |  Genitalia = external reproductive organs
Key Reproductive Terms
Coitus / Copulation — sexual intercourse
Conception — fertilization; union of sperm and egg
Spermatogenesis — production of sperm (sperm + -genesis = production)
Spermatozoon — single mature sperm cell; pl. spermatozoa
Testosterone — primary male sex hormone; from Leydig cells
Semen — fluid containing sperm + secretions from glands
Organs at a Glance
Testes (orchid/o, test/o) Epididymis (epididym/o) Ductus deferens (vas/o) Seminal vesicles (vesicul/o) Prostate (prostat/o) Urethra (urethr/o) Scrotum (scrot/o) Penis (pen/i, phall/o) Glans penis (balan/o) Prepuce/foreskin (preputi/o)
Male Reproductive Organs — Structure & Function
1
Testes (singular: testis)orchid/o, orchi/o, orch/o, test/o
Male gonads; located in the scrotum. Two functions: (1) Spermatogenesis — sperm production in seminiferous tubules; (2) Testosterone secretion — by Leydig (interstitial) cells. Optimal sperm production requires temperature ~2–3°C below body temperature — hence external scrotum.
2
Scrotumscrot/o
External pouch of skin containing and protecting the testes. Regulates temperature via cremaster muscle (raises/lowers testes).
3
Epididymisepididym/o
Coiled tube on the posterior of each testis where sperm mature and are stored. Sperm gain motility here. The head (caput), body (corpus), and tail (cauda) lead to the ductus deferens.
4
Ductus Deferens (Vas Deferens)vas/o
Muscular tube (~45 cm) that carries sperm from the epididymis to the ejaculatory duct. Passes through the inguinal canal in the spermatic cord. Cut during vasectomy.
5
Seminal Vesiclesvesicul/o, semin/i
Pair of glands posterior to the bladder. Produce ~60% of semen volume — fructose-rich fluid that nourishes and activates sperm. Merge with ductus deferens to form the ejaculatory duct.
6
Prostate Glandprostat/o
Walnut-sized gland surrounding the urethra below the bladder. Produces alkaline prostatic secretion (~30% semen) that neutralizes vaginal acidity, protecting sperm. Contains PSA (prostate-specific antigen).
7
Penispen/i, phall/o
Organ of copulation and urination. Contains corpus cavernosum (×2) and corpus spongiosum (surrounds urethra). Glans penis (balan/o) = sensitive distal tip. Prepuce/foreskin (preputi/o) = retractable skin fold covering glans.
Sperm Pathway — From Production to Ejaculation
Memory aid: "SEVEN UP" — Seminiferous tubules → Epididymis → Vas deferens → Ejaculatory duct → Nothing → Urethra → Penis
1
Seminiferous Tubules
Spermatogenesis: sperm produced here within the testes via meiosis. Takes ~72 days. Sertoli cells support developing sperm; Leydig cells produce testosterone.
2
Epididymisepididym/o
Sperm mature here for 2–12 days; gain motility and the ability to fertilize. Stored in the tail until ejaculation.
3
Ductus Deferens (Vas Deferens)vas/o
Peristaltic contractions propel sperm upward through the inguinal canal. The spermatic cord contains the vas deferens, testicular artery, veins, lymphatics, and nerves.
4
Ejaculatory Duct
Formed by the union of the ductus deferens and seminal vesicle duct. Passes through the prostate to empty into the urethra. Seminal fluid (fructose) added here.
5
Prostate Glandprostat/o
Alkaline prostatic fluid added to semen as it passes through the prostatic urethra. Neutralizes vaginal acidity to protect sperm.
6
Urethraurethr/o
Shared passage for urine and semen (never simultaneously). Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands add pre-ejaculatory fluid to lubricate the urethra and neutralize remaining urine acidity.
7
Urinary Meatus
Semen is ejaculated through the external urethral opening. Average ejaculate: ~3–5 mL semen containing 200–500 million spermatozoa.
Semen Composition
Seminal vesicles (~60%) — fructose, prostaglandins; nourishes sperm
Prostate gland (~30%) — alkaline fluid; PSA; liquefies semen
Bulbourethral glands (~5%) — clear lubricating fluid
Sperm (~5%) — spermatozoa from testes/epididymis
PSA (prostate-specific antigen) is a protein produced by prostate cells. Elevated PSA may indicate BPH, prostatitis, or prostate cancer.
Spermatic Cord Contents
Ductus deferens Testicular artery Pampiniform venous plexus Lymphatic vessels Autonomic nerves Cremaster muscle fibers
The spermatic cord passes through the inguinal canal — the same pathway relevant in inguinal hernia. Twisting of the spermatic cord = testicular torsion — surgical emergency.
Cryptorchidism
Undescended testis. crypt- (hidden) + orchid/o (testis) + -ism. Failure of one or both testes to descend from abdomen into scrotum before birth. Increases risk of infertility and testicular cancer. Treatment: orchiopexy before age 2.
Anorchism
Absence of one or both testes. an- (without) + orchid/o + -ism. Congenital (absent) or acquired (removed surgically = orchiectomy).
Testitis / Orchitis
Inflammation of a testis. test/o + -itis or orchi/o + -itis. Causes: mumps virus (mumps orchitis), bacterial infection, STI. Can lead to infertility.
Epididymitis
Inflammation of the epididymis. epididym/o + -itis. Most common cause of scrotal pain in adult males. Causes: Chlamydia, gonorrhea, E. coli. Symptoms: scrotal pain, swelling, fever.
Vesiculitis
Inflammation of the seminal vesicles. vesicul/o + -itis. Often associated with prostatitis; pelvic pain, hematospermia (blood in semen).
Testicular torsion
Twisting of the spermatic cord cutting off blood supply; sudden severe scrotal pain; SURGICAL EMERGENCY — must be treated within 4–6 hours to save the testis.
Varicocele
Enlargement of pampiniform venous plexus in the scrotum; "bag of worms" appearance; most common correctable cause of male infertility; increases testicular temperature.
Testicular carcinoma
Most common cancer in males aged 15–35; often presents as painless scrotal mass; highly curable (>95% cure rate); treated by orchiectomy + chemo/radiation.
Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)
hyper- (excessive) + -plasia (growth). Non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate; compresses urethra. Symptoms: LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) — weak stream, frequency, nocturia, urgency, incomplete emptying. Treatment: alpha-blockers, 5-alpha reductase inhibitors, TURP.
Prostatitis
Inflammation of the prostate. prostat/o + -itis. Types: acute bacterial (fever, dysuria, perineal pain), chronic bacterial, chronic pelvic pain syndrome (most common). Treated with antibiotics (bacterial types).
Prostate carcinoma
Most common cancer in males overall; arises from peripheral zone; often asymptomatic early; detected by elevated PSA or DRE; Gleason grading; treated by prostatectomy, radiation, hormone therapy.
PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)
Enzyme produced by prostate cells; measured by blood test; elevated in BPH, prostatitis, or prostate cancer; used for screening and monitoring.
BPH vs. prostate cancer: BPH is benign and affects the central/transitional zone; prostate cancer arises from the peripheral zone. Both can elevate PSA. Digital rectal exam (DRE) helps distinguish.
Hypospadias
Congenital — urethral opening on the underside (ventral surface) of the penis instead of the tip. hypo- (below) + -spadias (opening). Most common congenital penile anomaly. Treated surgically in infancy.
Epispadias
Urethral opening on the upper/dorsal surface of the penis. epi- (upon) + -spadias. Less common than hypospadias. Requires surgical repair.
Phimosis
Inability to retract the foreskin (prepuce) over the glans penis. Can cause pain, infection, urinary obstruction. Treatment: topical steroids or circumcision.
Balanitis
Inflammation of the glans penis. balan/o + -itis. Often from infection (bacterial/fungal) or poor hygiene; more common in uncircumcised males.
Erectile dysfunction (ED)
Inability to achieve or maintain an erection sufficient for sexual intercourse. Causes: vascular, neurological, hormonal, or psychological. Treatment: PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil/Viagra), penile implants, vacuum devices.
Nongonococcal urethritis (NGU)
Urethral inflammation NOT caused by gonorrhea. Most common cause: Chlamydia trachomatis. Symptoms: urethral discharge, dysuria. Treatment: azithromycin or doxycycline.
Azoospermia
Absence of sperm in semen. a- (without) + zo/o (life/animal) + sperm/o + -ia. Causes: obstruction (post-vasectomy), testicular failure, hormonal. Leading cause of male infertility.
Oligospermia
Low sperm count. olig/o (few) + sperm/o + -ia. Defined as <15 million sperm/mL; reduces fertility. Causes: varicocele, infection, heat exposure, toxins, hormonal.
Plethysmography
plethysm/o + -graphy. Measurement of volume changes in an organ; in reproductive medicine, penile plethysmography measures erectile function and blood flow.
Male infertility evaluation: Semen analysis is the primary test — measures sperm count, motility, morphology, and volume. Azoospermia = no sperm; oligospermia = too few; asthenospermia = poor motility; teratospermia = abnormal shape.
Human Papillomavirus (HPV)
Most common STI; many strains; causes genital warts (condylomata acuminata) and is associated with penile, anal, and oropharyngeal cancers. Preventable by vaccine (Gardasil).
Gonorrhea
Caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae; purulent urethral discharge, dysuria; can cause epididymitis, prostatitis; treated with ceftriaxone.
Chlamydia
Caused by Chlamydia trachomatis; most reportable STI; often asymptomatic in males; can cause NGU, epididymitis; treated with azithromycin/doxycycline.
Syphilis
Caused by Treponema pallidum; stages: primary (painless chancre) → secondary (rash) → latent → tertiary; treated with penicillin.
Herpes simplex virus (HSV-2)
Genital herpes; painful vesicular lesions; recurrent; no cure but managed with antivirals (acyclovir, valacyclovir).
HPV vaccination is recommended for males through age 26 (catch-up through age 45 in some cases). Reduces risk of genital warts and HPV-related cancers.
Diagnostic Procedures
PSA test
Prostate-specific antigen blood test; elevated in BPH, prostatitis, prostate cancer; used for screening and monitoring
Digital rectal exam (DRE)
Physical palpation of prostate via rectum; assesses size, texture, nodules; distinguishes BPH from cancer
Semen analysis
Evaluates sperm count, motility, morphology, volume; primary test for male infertility workup
Plethysmography (plethysm/o + -graphy)
Measures blood flow and volume changes; penile plethysmography evaluates erectile function
Testicular ultrasound
Imaging to evaluate scrotal masses, torsion, varicocele, epididymitis; no radiation; first-line for scrotal pathology
Prostate biopsy (TRUS-guided)
Transrectal ultrasound-guided needle biopsy of prostate; performed when PSA elevated or DRE abnormal; definitive prostate cancer diagnosis
Urethral culture / urinalysis
Diagnoses UTI, NGU, STIs; swab culture identifies organism and antibiotic sensitivity
Therapeutic Procedures
Testis / scrotum
Orchiopexy
orchid/o + -pexy; surgical fixation of undescended testis into scrotum; treats cryptorchidism; done before age 2
Orchiectomy
orchid/o + -ectomy; surgical removal of a testis; treats testicular cancer; radical orchiectomy = entire testis + spermatic cord
Vas deferens
Vasectomy (vas/o + -ectomy)
Surgical cutting and sealing of the vas deferens; permanent male contraception; does NOT affect testosterone or ejaculation volume
Vasovasostomy (vas/o + -stomy)
Surgical reconnection of the vas deferens; vasectomy reversal; vas/o + vas/o + -stomy (new opening)
Prostate
Prostatectomy (prostat/o + -ectomy)
Surgical removal of the prostate; for prostate cancer; radical = entire prostate + seminal vesicles
TURP — Transurethral resection of prostate
trans- + urethr/o + resection; endoscopic removal of prostatic tissue via urethra; treats BPH without external incision
Penis
Circumcision (circum- + -cision)
Surgical removal of the foreskin (prepuce); circum- (around) + -cision (cutting); treats phimosis; common neonatal procedure
Hypospadias repair
Urethroplasty to reposition urethral opening to correct location at tip of glans; done in infancy
